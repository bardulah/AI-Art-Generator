| **99.8% fidelity** | 2027 | Very High | 30-35% | Critical - makes 1,000 qubits usable |

**Why 1,000-Qubit Target is High-Risk:**

1. **Multi-Chip Coherence Challenge:**
   - 1,000 qubits = 12-15 chips @ 60-80 qubits each
   - Requires ultra-low-loss quantum interconnects (photonic links or superconducting buses)
   - **State-of-the-art:** IBM demonstrated 3-chip coherence (127 qubits × 3); 12-chip is 4x harder
   - **Rigetti progress:** 36-qubit multi-chip demonstrated (2025); 10x scale-up needed in 2 years

2. **Cryogenic Scalability:**
   - 1,000 qubits need refrigeration to 10-15 millikelvin (0.01-0.015 Kelvin)
   - Current dilution fridges max out at ~500 qubits
   - **Solution:** Custom fridge OR modular architecture (multiple fridges interconnected) = unproven

3. **Control Electronics:**
   - 1,000 qubits = 5,000+ control lines (5 per qubit: gate, readout, flux, etc.)
   - Signal integrity, crosstalk, synchronization at nanosecond precision = massive engineering challenge
   - **IBM/Google approach:** Custom ASIC controllers (took 3-5 years to develop)

4. **Error Budget:**
   - 99.8% fidelity = 0.2% error rate per gate
   - 1,000-qubit algorithm with 100 gates = 20% cumulative error (unusable without quantum error correction)
   - **QECC overhead:** 10-100 physical qubits per logical qubit → 1,000 qubits = 10-100 logical qubits
   - **Implication:** Even if 1,000 qubits delivered, effective qubit count may be 50-100 (vs IonQ's 36 at 99.99%)

**Competitor Benchmarks:**
- **IBM:** 10 years to reach 1,121 qubits (Condor), 15+ years of superconducting R&D
- **Google:** 8 years to reach 70 qubits (Willow) with breakthrough error correction
- **Rigetti:** 12 years (2013-2025) to reach 84 qubits; targeting 1,000 in 15 years total = aggressive
